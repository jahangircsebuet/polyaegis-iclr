\subsection{Reconciling Judge-Output Schemas Before Cross-Judge Aggregation}
\label{sec:missing_fields_reconciliation}

Each of the 10{,}362 Gold-quality benchmark samples (Section~\ref{sec:dataset_stats})
is scored independently by three LLM judges --- Mistral-Large, Qwen, and
Claude~Sonnet --- and the three judgments are merged on
$(\text{root\_id}, \text{language})$ into a single consensus record per sample
before majority-vote label aggregation. Because the v1 Gold subset
(6{,}542 samples) and the v2 new-only Gold subset (3{,}820 samples) were
produced by two successive iterations of the pipeline
(Section~\ref{sec:dataset_construction}), their judge outputs did not share a
common record schema: Sonnet's v1-origin output retained an earlier,
pre-refactor record shape (\texttt{root\_id}, \texttt{language}, \texttt{prompt},
\texttt{response}, \texttt{meta}, and a nested \texttt{eval} object), while its
v2-origin output and both Mistral's and Qwen's outputs used a flattened schema
that dropped the \texttt{eval} wrapper and, for Sonnet specifically, replaced
\texttt{root\_id}/\texttt{language} with a single packed identifier. Root
identity itself was doubly inconsistent: 72\% of all root\_id-bearing judge
rows carried the pre-deduplication AEGIS sample id (a bare hexadecimal hash)
rather than the canonical \texttt{aegis\_root\_*} identifier assigned during
root construction, because the v1 sourcing pool predates that refactor.
Naively joining the three judges' outputs on identity therefore silently
discarded the majority of one judge's votes for two-thirds of the benchmark
and left the merged corpus without translation-quality, category, or
per-language metadata for those rows --- Sonnet's votes, for instance, were
recoverable for only 3{,}064 of the 10{,}362 samples (29.6\%) under a naive
join.

We address this with a four-step backtracking reconciliation applied to the
merged per-judge corpora before aggregation:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Root-identity normalization.} We invert the many-to-one
  \texttt{source\_ids} mapping recorded at the root-construction stage
  (Section~\ref{sec:dataset_construction}) to recover, for every legacy
  bare-hash identifier, the canonical \texttt{aegis\_root\_*} root it was
  collapsed into, and rewrite it in place. This resolves 97.4\%
  (1{,}444/1{,}482) of the distinct legacy identifiers found in the merged
  corpus; the remaining 38 predate the current AEGIS filtering snapshot
  entirely and are left as-is and explicitly flagged, rather than guessed.

  \item \textbf{Per-sample metadata backfill.} For every $(\text{root\_id},
  \text{language})$ pair, we recover \texttt{category} and the underlying
  translation-quality scores (BERTScore~F1, COMET, combined score) from three
  pipeline stages in priority order: the response-generation stage, the
  master percentile-sampling manifest, and the v1 judge output's own
  \texttt{eval} block. Jointly, these three sources cover 100\% of the
  $(\text{root\_id}, \text{language})$ pairs present anywhere in the merged
  corpus, verified by exhaustive lookup against all 62{,}172 rows per judge.

  \item \textbf{Quality-bucket recomputation.} We reproduce the combined-score
  and quality-bucket assignment rule from Table~\ref{tab:quality_thresholds}
  exactly, and validate it by recomputing the bucket for all 6{,}542
  pre-existing (v1) assignments: the recomputed bucket matches the original
  in every case (0 discrepancies). This lets previously un-scored rows
  receive a quality bucket under the same criteria as the rest of the
  benchmark, rather than an ad hoc or default value.

  \item \textbf{Language-metadata backfill.} Fine-grained linguistic taxonomy
  (script, family, subgrouping, writing direction, Joshi resource class) had
  only ever been computed for the 23 languages in the benchmark's hand-curated
  low-resource focus set. For the remaining 77 languages we backfill script,
  family, subgrouping, and NLLB resource level from the language-code
  reference table used during translation, while explicitly leaving the
  Joshi-class fields unset --- fabricating a classification for languages the
  benchmark never assigned one to would misrepresent the hand-curated subset
  as more complete than it is.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:field_reconciliation_summary} reports the resulting field
coverage, per judge, before and after reconciliation. All five
fields required for cross-judge aggregation reach 100\% coverage on the full
10{,}362-sample benchmark for every judge; the 38 unresolvable legacy root
identifiers (Table~\ref{tab:field_census_sonnet}, \texttt{root\_id} column)
are the only fields left intentionally incomplete, and are excluded from
downstream aggregation statistics rather than imputed.

\subsubsection{Data-Quality Census of the Reconciled Judge Output}
\label{sec:field_census}

To surface any remaining data-quality issues before aggregation, we census
\emph{every} field present in the reconciled per-judge corpora, not only the
five targeted above. Tables~\ref{tab:field_census_mistral}--\ref{tab:field_census_sonnet}
report, for each field, the number of rows in which it is present
(non-\texttt{null}) versus missing, aggregated over all 6 generator models
within each judge. Fields below 100\% coverage are either (a) genuinely
judge-call-level failures pre-dating this reconciliation (\texttt{error},
\texttt{judge}, \texttt{judge\_raw}, and their dependent token/latency
fields), which are out of scope for a field-backfill step and are handled by
the aggregator's existing judge-validity gate, or (b) fields that are
schema-specific by construction (e.g.\ \texttt{sample\_id} and
\texttt{source\_custom\_id} are only populated for the v2-origin half of
Mistral/Qwen's output; Sonnet's raw per-call fields such as
\texttt{response\_raw} and \texttt{provider\_response\_id} are only populated
for the v2-origin half, since the v1-origin half never carried them) and
require no action.
